\begin{center}\begin{minipage}{0.97\textwidth}\small
\textbf{Abstract.} ``Matrix SYK'' models are purely fermionic $U(N)$ matrix quantum mechanics with a cubic supercharge, $Q=\sum C_{abc}\Tr\Psi^a\Psi^b\Psi^c$ and $H=\{Q,\bQ\}$. The three-flavour version proposed by Chen is the simplest candidate for a matrix model whose BPS sector is \emph{fortuitous} and \emph{R-charge concentrated} --- the two properties that, in $\mathcal N=2$ SYK, give rise to a super-Schwarzian low-energy description and to black-hole-like counting of BPS microstates. The model has a finite Hilbert space, so exact diagonalisation settles everything at $N=2$ and almost nothing beyond, and its interesting ground states have no classical large-$N$ saddle. We are developing the quantum-mechanical matrix bootstrap as the tool for $N>2$. So far we have: a sector-resolved bootstrap that is exact for the single-matrix model up to $N=4$ and for the three-matrix model at $N=2$ in every sector we have tested; a reformulation in terms of trace words whose cost does not grow with $N$, which establishes $E_0(k;N)=16N^3-(15+38k)N$ for the low sectors $k\le N-1$ and gives the first rigorous bound in an interacting sector at $N=3$; an exact refined Witten index that reproduces the Turiaci--Witten $\cos(\pi k/3)$ BPS profile and shows $e^{cN^2}$ multiplicities per irrep; and a ``BPS-exclusion'' bootstrap that rules out BPS states sector by sector in seconds at any $N$, currently reaching half-way to the BPS window. Index and exclusion together are a concrete route to establishing R-charge concentration at $N=3$ and $4$.
\end{minipage}\end{center}

\section{Introduction}

\paragraph{The models.}
The degrees of freedom are complex fermionic $N\times N$ matrices $\Psi^a_{ij}$, with $a=1,\dots,p$ a flavour index, obeying $\{\Psi^a_{ij},\bPsi^b_{kl}\}=\delta^{ab}\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$; $U(N)$ acts by conjugation. Everything is generated by a nilpotent cubic supercharge:
\begin{equation}
Q=\sum_{a,b,c}C_{abc}\,\Tr\!\big[\Psi^a\Psi^b\Psi^c\big],\qquad H=\{Q,\bQ\}\ \ge0,\qquad [N_\Psi,Q]=3Q,\quad N_\Psi=\Tr\Psi^a\bPsi^a .
\end{equation}
The fermion number $N_\Psi$ plays the role of the R-charge, and $Q$ raises it by three. For a single flavour, $Q=\Tr\Psi^3$, this is Chen's model~\cite{Chen:2025fortuity}, and it is exactly solvable: the Hamiltonian is a function of the gauge Casimir, $H=3N(N^2-1)-9\hat C_2$, and the BPS states (those with $E=0$) occupy a window of $N+1$ consecutive R-charges, $N_\Psi\in[\tfrac{N(N-1)}2,\tfrac{N(N+1)}2]$. With three flavours and Chen's couplings $C_{abc}$ (the cyclic symmetrisation of the indicator function of $a\le b\le c$) the Casimir structure is lost. We derived the exact single-trace form of $H$ for this case --- a nine-term expression that also contains a $\Tr\Psi^c\Tr\bPsi^d$ piece coupling the trace modes --- and verified that it is \emph{not} a function of Casimirs. This ``three-matrix model'' is our target.

\paragraph{Why it is interesting.}
In $\mathcal N=2$ SYK~\cite{Fu:2016SUSYSYK} the BPS states are concentrated in a window of $O(1)$ R-charges around half filling, with counts following $\cos(\pi k/3)$. This is the fingerprint of the $\mathcal N=2$ super-Schwarzian~\cite{Turiaci:2023N2JT} and, in the fortuity picture of Chang and Lin~\cite{Chang:2024covering,Chang:2024fortuitySYK}, of a BPS sector built from black-hole-like ``fortuitous'' states: states with $e^{cN^2}$ multiplicities that no large-$N$ saddle sees. The single-matrix model fails this test in two ways --- its window grows with $N$, and its BPS states form one enormous irrep with $O(1)$ multiplicity. The three-matrix model at $N=2$, by contrast, has 972 BPS states in exactly three sectors, $N_\Psi\in\{5,6,7\}$, with counts $243{:}486{:}243$: the $1{:}2{:}1$ SYK pattern. What happens for $N\ge3$ is unknown.

\paragraph{Research questions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item[Q1.] Does the three-matrix model exhibit R-charge concentration for $N\ge3$: an $O(1)$ window, $\cos(\pi k/3)$ counts, and $e^{cN^2}$ multiplicities per irrep?
\item[Q2.] Is it chaotic? The natural diagnostic is the Altland--Zirnbauer statistics of the singular values of $Q$ between adjacent sectors.
\item[Q3.] Is three the ``magic number'' --- the minimal flavour structure that breaks the Casimir shortcut and introduces genuine R-charge dynamics and chaos?
\item[Q4.] What are the energies of the near-BPS sectors, and how do they scale with $N$? The super-Schwarzian predicts gaps growing like $q^2$ with the distance from the window.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What makes these models distinctive, and why a bootstrap.}
There are no bosons. The Hilbert space is the $2^{pN^2}$-dimensional Fock space, every operator algebra truncates, and in principle everything is finite-dimensional linear algebra. In practice, $2^{27}$ at $N=3$ already puts the full spectrum out of reach (only sectors with a few particles, $k\le4$, can still be diagonalised exactly), and $2^{48}$ at $N=4$ ends exact diagonalisation for good. At the same time, the states we care about have no classical large-$N$ description: fortuitous states carry maximal Casimir and are as far from gauge singlets as possible, so the standard planar bootstrap --- which assumes factorisation --- is blind to exactly this physics. A \emph{rigorous, finite-$N$} bootstrap, producing lower bounds on sector ground energies and infeasibility certificates for BPS states, is therefore the natural tool here, and, we will argue, a promising one.

\section{Methodology}

\paragraph{The sector-resolved bootstrap at finite $N$.}
The basic object is the ground energy $E_0(k)$ of the sector with $N_\Psi=k$. Rather than a state, we optimise over linear functionals $\phi(O)=\Tr[\rho_k P_kOP_k]$, where $\rho_k$ is a Hermitian matrix on the $k$-block that is \emph{not} assumed positive. The optimisation problem is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;\begin{gathered}
\min_{\rho_k=\rho_k^\dagger}\ \phi(H)\qquad\text{subject to}\\[3pt]
\phi(\mathbf 1)=1,\qquad
\big[\phi(\Tr w_a^\dagger w_b)\big]_{ab}\succeq0,\qquad
\big[\phi((\Tr w_a)^\dagger\Tr w_b)\big]_{ab}\succeq0,\qquad
\phi([H,X])=0,\qquad \phi((N_\Psi-k)X)=0 .
\end{gathered}\;}
\label{eq:sdp}
\end{equation}
Here $w$ runs over \emph{open} (adjoint-valued) words in the letters $\Psi^a,\bPsi^a$ up to some length $L$, which we call the level. The two matrix inequalities are positivity of the Gram matrix of the states $w_{ij}|\psi\rangle$ in its adjoint and singlet channels; the equalities are the equations of motion and the statement that the functional lives in sector $k$, imposed for every operator $X$ that appears in the cones. Every constraint is satisfied by the true sector ground state, so the minimum is a rigorous lower bound on $E_0(k)$, and it can only improve as $L$ grows. The dual problem produces a sum-of-squares certificate $H-E\succeq0$ on the sector --- the direct analogue of the functional in the conformal bootstrap.

Two ingredients turned out to matter most. The first is the sector projection itself: charged operators have $\phi=0$, and commutators act within the block. The second is symmetry: $\rho_k$ may be taken invariant under $SU(N)_{\rm gauge}\times\mathbb Z_3$ at no cost (group averaging), and once it is, the singlet/adjoint decomposition of the Gram matrix introduced by Cho et al.~\cite{Cho:2024thermal} becomes available, $\phi(\Tr w_a^\dagger w_b)-\tfrac1N\phi(\Tr w_a^\dagger\Tr w_b)\succeq0$. We implement the invariance through an isotypic decomposition of the block, $\rho_k=\oplus_R\sigma_R\otimes\mathbf 1$, which leaves $D=\sum_Rm_R^2$ parameters instead of $d_k^2$ --- at most $90$ unknowns at $N=2$, $k=2$, and $666$ at $k=3$. The cost is nevertheless set by the block dimension $d_k$ (at most $924$ at $N=2$, but up to $2\times10^7$ at $N=3$), so this version of the bootstrap is a validation tool rather than the route to large $N$.

\paragraph{An exact finite-$N$ formulation in terms of traces.}
To go beyond $N=2$ we re-express the same constraints as identities among expectation values of \emph{trace words}, $\phi(\Tr w_1\cdots\Tr w_m)$, with $N$ entering only as a numerical coefficient. Only two facts about the fermionic matrix algebra are needed. Moving a letter cyclically around a trace preserves the matrix structure and produces double traces,
\begin{equation}
\Tr[\ell_1\ell_2\cdots\ell_L]=(-1)^{L-1}\Tr[\ell_2\cdots\ell_L\ell_1]+\sum_{m}(-1)^m\,\delta_{\ell_1\bar\ell_m}\,\Tr[\ell_2\cdots\ell_{m-1}]\,\Tr[\ell_{m+1}\cdots\ell_L],
\end{equation}
whereas swapping two adjacent letters inside a trace does not preserve it --- so no normal ordering is ever performed. The important point is what we do \emph{not} do: we impose no large-$N$ factorisation. Multi-trace expectation values are kept as independent unknowns, so every bound is exact at the chosen integer $N$, and the planar limit --- which would wash out the $1/N$-suppressed concentration physics --- is never taken. The sector condition becomes the linear family $\phi((N_\Psi-k)X)=0$, and the finite-$N$ trace relations (the vanishing antisymmetriser over $N+1$ indices) are optional rows that turn out to matter only at $N=2$. The resulting semidefinite programme is built once, evaluated at any $N$ one likes, and handed to an interior-point (Clarabel) or first-order (SCS) solver. Its size is set by the level, not by $N$ and not by $k$.

\paragraph{Complementary tools.}
Three smaller pieces round out the toolkit. A \emph{few-body exact diagonalisation} works directly in the sector $N_\Psi=k$, on the basis of $k$-subsets of the $3N^2$ fermionic modes, without ever forming the Fock space; the sector dimension is $\binom{3N^2}{k}$ (for instance $351$ for $N=3,k=2$ and $17\,550$ for $N=3,k=4$), so it gives exact ground energies for a few particles at moderate $N$ --- precisely where the trace bootstrap operates --- and nothing near half filling. An exact \emph{refined Witten index}, resolved by $N_\Psi \bmod 3$, $U(N)$ irrep and $\mathbb Z_3$ flavour charge, is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. And the BPS-exclusion variant of \eqref{eq:sdp} is described there and in Sec.~\ref{sec:outlook}.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Single-matrix model, where the answer is known.}
At level 2 the sector bootstrap reproduces the exact Casimir ladder in all ten sectors at $N=3$ ($72,45,18,0,0,0,0,18,45,72$) and in 15 of the 17 sectors at $N=4$. The two exceptions at $N=4$ are the sectors adjacent to the BPS window, $k=5$ and $k=11$, whose true energy is $18$; there the bound climbs with level, $0,\,0,\,11.4,\,14.7$ at levels $2$ to $5$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:conv}a). An ablation at $N=3$ shows what each ingredient does: the sector restriction fixes $k=1$, the adjoint channel fixes $k=2$, the supercharges in the singlet cone supply the SUSY floor $E\ge0$, and ground-state positivity is not needed.

\paragraph{Three-matrix model at $N=2$.}
Table~\ref{tab:N2} summarises the sector ground energies. Every sector with a positive gap, except the almost-BPS $k=4$, is reproduced \emph{exactly}: $k=2$ at level 3 and $k=3$ at level 4, while the BPS window $\{5,6,7\}$ is exact automatically through the SUSY floor. Two lessons carry over to everything that follows: a lifted sector becomes tight one level after it leaves the floor, and the singlet/adjoint split is what closes the gap (with the plain adjoint channel the $k=2$ bound stalls at $4.55$). The bootstrap also produces \emph{allowed regions}. Fixing $\phi(H)=E$ and extremising an observable maps out an island in the $(E,\langle\hat C_2\rangle)$ plane that encloses the exact spectrum (Fig.~\ref{fig:island}); for the single-matrix model the island degenerates to the exact Casimir line. With the eigenstate constraints $\phi(OH)=E\,\phi(O)$ added, the level-2 problem becomes feasible \emph{only at the exact eigenvalues} ($5.16536$, $12$, $13.4907$, each to $10^{-8}$) --- an archipelago rather than an island.

\paragraph{Beyond $N=2$ with the trace formulation.}
A single constraint set, evaluated at $N=2,3,4,5,10,30,100$, reproduces the analytic anchors $E_0(0)=16N^3-15N$ and $E_0(1)=16N^3-53N$ to eight digits. The new results are collected in Table~\ref{tab:largeN}. The level-2 bound is \emph{tight} for $k=2$ at every $N\ge3$ and for $k=3$ at every $N\ge4$, and in all these cases it equals $16N^3-(15+38k)N$; few-body ED confirms the values wherever it can ($N\le5$). Since a simple variational state gives the same energy from above, this establishes
\begin{equation}
E_0(k;N)=16N^3-(15+38k)N\qquad\text{for }k\le N-1\ \text{(all cases tested)}.
\end{equation}
In words: the $k$-particle sector is \emph{free} as long as $k\le N-1$. The lowest one-particle multiplet is an $SU(N)$ adjoint, and $k$ antisymmetrised copies of it, in the component of $\wedge^k\mathbf{adj}$ with the largest Casimir, are annihilated by the quartic interaction. We checked this explicitly at $N=3$: $\wedge^2\mathbf 8=\mathbf{10}\oplus\overline{\mathbf{10}}\oplus\mathbf 8$, and the 20-dimensional part is free while the adjoint is lifted by $+100$. The first sector where interactions act is therefore $k=N$. There the bootstrap gives the first rigorous bound in an interacting sector beyond $N=2$: $E_0(3;3)\ge70.35$ at level 3, against the exact value $75.79$ from few-body ED in the $2925$-dimensional sector (level 2 gives the free value $45$).



\paragraph{An analytic complement: the refined index.}
Because $Q$ raises $N_\Psi$ by three, the sectors organise into three strands, $\mathcal H_c\to\mathcal H_{c+3}\to\mathcal H_{c+6}\to\cdots$ with $c=k\bmod3$, each further split by $U(N)$ irrep $\lambda$ and $\mathbb Z_3$ flavour charge $\omega$. The Euler characteristic of a strand --- the alternating sum of its sector dimensions --- is a purely kinematic quantity, and its modulus is a lower bound on the number of BPS multiplets in that strand, with equality exactly when the BPS states of the strand sit at a single R-charge. This ``index saturation'' is the same statement as R-charge concentration~\cite{Chang:2024fortuitySYK}. Using $\mathbb Z_3$-Fourier flavour letters, the Fock character $\prod_{m}\prod_{i,j}(1+t z^m u_i/u_j)$ gives every such index exactly. At $N=2$ the index equals the ED count in all 36 strands. The totals per strand have the closed form
\begin{equation}
I_c(N)=\tfrac23\,3^{3N^2/2}\cos\!\Big(\tfrac{2\pi c}3+\tfrac{\pi N^2}2\Big),
\end{equation}
which is \emph{exactly} the Turiaci--Witten profile $\cos(\pi(k-k_*)/3)$ about half filling: $2{:}1{:}1$ for even $N$ and $0{:}1{:}1$ for odd $N$. At $N=3$ the index vanishes in every $k\equiv0$ strand. The multiplicities per irrep grow like $e^{0.75N^2}$ ($18,\,891,\,3.5\times10^5$ at $N=2,3,4$) and include gauge singlets ($6,\,81,\,1944$). The single-matrix model, put through the same computation, has a single irrep per strand with multiplicity at most $9$ and no singlets at all --- the fortuitous/black-hole-like dichotomy of Chang and Lin, now made quantitative.

\paragraph{BPS exclusion (preliminary).}
A BPS state satisfies $Q\psi=\bQ\psi=0$, and therefore the linear relations $\phi(XQ)=\phi(QX)=\phi(X\bQ)=\phi(\bQ X)=0$ for every operator $X$. Adding these rows to \eqref{eq:sdp} and maximising a feasibility margin turns the bootstrap into a certificate of \emph{absence}: a negative margin means the sector contains no BPS state. At $N=2$ this excludes $k\le2$ at level 2 and $k\le3$ at level 3 --- one level earlier than the energy bound each time. At $N=3$ and $N=4$, level 2 already excludes $k\le6$ and $k\le11$ in seconds, and level 3 adds $k=12$ at $N=4$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:conv}b). Since the index fixes the BPS count of each strand, emptying every R-charge but one by exclusion would determine the BPS spectrum completely.

\section{Outlook}\label{sec:outlook}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Concentration at $N=3,4$ from index and exclusion together.} The index says the $k\equiv0$ strands are empty at $N=3$ and fixes the counts of the others; exclusion currently empties $k\le6$ at $N=3$ and $k\le12$ at $N=4$, about half-way to the window. Two natural next steps: irrep-resolved exclusion (the quadratic Casimir is a trace polynomial, hence a linear constraint), which needs to target only the irreps that actually carry index; and exact cohomology ranks at $N=3$, computed sparsely over a prime field, as an independent check.
\item \textbf{Near-BPS energies as a function of $N$.} The level-3 trace engine gives rigorous, $N$-dependent bounds on the interaction energy of the first interacting sector $k=N$ in minutes per $N$; calibrated against few-body ED at $N=4$ (dimension $1.9\times10^5$), this is a direct test of the super-Schwarzian scaling.
\item \textbf{Sparse diagonalisation at $N=3$.} The sectors near the window have dimensions up to $2\times10^7$, feasible with a matrix-free Lanczos implementation: energies, exact BPS counts, and the singular-value statistics of $Q_k$ for the chaos question.
\item \textbf{Analytic work.} A proof of the free-sector formula (a short Casimir argument), large-$N$ asymptotics of the per-irrep index from a saddle point of the character integral, and the traceless $su(N)$ and two-flavour variants, which bear on the ``magic number'' question.
\item \textbf{Solvers.} The level-4 problem is assembled but not yet solved to tolerance (see the appendix). The dual, SDPA/SDPB-style form after eliminating the equalities, or the reversal $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry that halves every cone, would bring it within reach of interior-point methods.
\end{itemize}

\section{Numerical implementation}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Finite-$N$ engine} (\texttt{sector\_bootstrap.py}). Open words are built as blocks between sectors, so the $2^{pN^2}$-dimensional space is never formed. An isotypic reducer maps every operator to its $D=\sum_Rm_R^2$ invariant components as soon as it is built, and the spans of the touched operators and of the equations of motion are accumulated as streaming $2D\times2D$ Gram matrices. A diagnostic that evaluates every constraint on the exact, multiplet-averaged ground state caught a round-off Hermitian part being normalised into a spurious constraint; all such cutoffs are now relative to $\|X\|$.
\item \textbf{Trace algebra} (\texttt{trace\_algebra.py}). One primitive --- swapping two adjacent letters in a \emph{routed term} (letters in operator order plus a routing permutation; the anticommutator branch rewires the routing, closed index loops give factors of $N$) --- generates canonical cyclic forms, products, daggers, commutators with $H$, self-symmetry reductions such as $\Tr[\ell\ell]=0$, and the finite-$N$ relations. Coefficients are polynomials in $N$. Every identity is checked against explicit operators at $N=2,3$ (a thousand random terms to $10^{-15}$; the algebra recovers $\{Q,\bQ\}=H$ on its own).
\item \textbf{Assembly and conditioning} (\texttt{trace\_bootstrap.py}). Variables are generated by closure; reality is imposed at the variable level (halving the unknowns); rows are unit-normalised; the unknowns carry a 't~Hooft scaling $N^{\sum(1+L_i/2)}$ and the objective is divided by $N^3$, without which nothing works beyond $N\approx30$. Solvers are called directly. Settings that matter: Clarabel with chordal decomposition off and equilibration on; SCS with $\rho_x=10^{-3}$, the Apple-Accelerate LDL backend and fixed scaling. A parallel fixed-$N$ build produces the level-4 problem (51k monomials, cones up to dimension 168) in 25 seconds.
\item \textbf{Cost and limits.} Levels 2 and 3 take seconds to minutes at any $N$ and at most 5~GB. Level 4 is solver-limited: interior-point codes keep a dense block per cone that grows like the square of its vectorised dimension (about 25~GB for a 168-dimensional complex cone), and the first-order solver stalls at $10^{-2}$ residuals. Every run is preceded by a memory estimate and guarded by a watchdog.
\item \textbf{Few-body ED and index} (\texttt{fewbody.py}, \texttt{bps\_index.py}). Trace operators act on $k$-subset basis states by explicit index sums. The index is exact integer arithmetic: the Fock generating function is built by integer shifts on the $SU(N)$ torus, and irrep multiplicities follow from the Weyl alternating sum; seconds for $N\le4$.
\end{itemize}
