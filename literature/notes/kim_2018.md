# Kim (2018) — Large N Tensor and SYK Models

**File:** `papers/kim_2018.pdf`

## Full citation

Jaewon Kim, *Large N Tensor and SYK Models*, A.B. senior thesis, Department of Physics, Princeton University
(advisor Igor Klebanov), arXiv:1811.04330 [hep-th], 11 Nov 2018. 62 pp. **An undergraduate thesis, not a
refereed paper**: its new results should be weighed accordingly. It builds directly on Klebanov–Tarnopolsky
(see `klebanov_milekhin_popov_tarnopolsky_2018.md` for the same group's later work).

## Main question

What is the large-$N$ (melonic) physics of a two-flavour Majorana tensor quantum mechanics with $O(N)^3$
symmetry, whose quartic Hamiltonian interpolates between coupled and decoupled flavours through a real
parameter $\beta$? Specifically: the propagator, the four-point kernels, the spectrum of scaling dimensions of
bilinear primaries, dualities between different $\beta$, and whether some $\beta$ are unstable.

## Physical system

- Two flavours of **real (Majorana)** fermions $\psi_i^{abc}$, $i=1,2$, each a rank-3 tensor with every index in
  the fundamental of a separate $O(N)$: symmetry $O(N)^3$ (eq. 18). Same number of degrees of freedom as one
  complex tensor, but only $O(N)^3$ rather than a larger symmetry.
- Quartic **tetrahedral** Hamiltonian (eq. 17):
  $$H=\tfrac{\beta g}{2}\{\text{three flavour-coupling tetrahedra }\psi_1\psi_1\psi_2\psi_2\}
  +\tfrac g4\{\psi_1^4+\psi_2^4\ \text{tetrahedra}\}.$$
  $\beta$ weights the flavour-coupling term against the decoupled one.
- **No supersymmetry**: no supercharge, no BPS states, no R-charge, no cohomology anywhere in the thesis.
- Large-$N$ limit with $g^2N^3$ fixed: **melonic** dominance (§1.2.1), as in Klebanov–Tarnopolsky and
  Gurau–Witten, which is what makes the model SYK-like without disorder.

## Important definitions

- Index-loop counting: a diagram with $\mathcal N$ closed index loops scales as $N^{\mathcal N}$; melons maximise
  loops at fixed vertex number (§1.2.1, Fig. 10–11).
- Bilinear primaries $O^{2n+1}_{1,2}=\psi_1\partial_t^{2n+1}\psi_1\pm\psi_2\partial_t^{2n+1}\psi_2$,
  $O^{2n}_3=\psi_1\partial_t^{2n}\psi_2-\psi_2\partial_t^{2n}\psi_1$,
  $O^{2n+1}_4=\psi_1\partial_t^{2n+1}\psi_2+\psi_2\partial_t^{2n+1}\psi_1$.
- Kernel eigenvalues $g_i(h)$; scaling dimensions are roots of $g_i(h)=1$.

## Main assumptions

Large $N$ with $g^2N^3$ fixed; strong coupling (IR), where the free term in the Schwinger–Dyson equation is
dropped; conformal ansatz for the propagator. Global (ungauged) $O(N)^3$; no singlet projection.

## Main analytical results

1. **Propagator** (eqs. 22, 112): the SD equation is SYK-like with effective coupling $g^2(3\beta^2+1)N^3$,
   giving $G(t)=-\big(4\pi(3\beta^2+1)g^2N^3\big)^{-1/4}\operatorname{sgn}(t)/|t|^{1/2}$, i.e. $\Delta=1/4$ as in
   $q=4$ SYK.
2. **Kernels and spectra** (eqs. 113–116):
   $g_1(h)=-\tfrac32\frac{\tan(\frac\pi2(h-\frac12))}{h-\frac12}$,
   $g_2(h)=-\tfrac32\frac{1-\beta^2}{3\beta^2+1}\frac{\tan(\frac\pi2(h-\frac12))}{h-\frac12}$,
   $g_3(h)=\frac{3\beta^2-3\beta}{3\beta^2+1}\frac{\tan(\frac\pi2(h+\frac12))}{h-\frac12}$,
   $g_4(h)=\frac{-3\beta^2+3\beta}{3\beta^2+1}\frac{\tan(\frac\pi2(h+\frac12))}{h-\frac12}$.
   Reported to match Gross–Rosenhaus two-flavour SYK and the Klebanov–Tarnopolsky tensor models.
3. **Duality** (§2.3, eq. 95): the flavour rotation $\psi_{1,2}=(\tilde\psi_1\pm\tilde\psi_2)/\sqrt2$ maps
   $H_\beta$ to $H_{\beta'}$ with $\beta'=\frac{1-\beta}{3\beta+1}$, $g'=\frac{(3\beta+1)g}{2}$. Not an exact
   equivalence since $g$ rescales, but energy ratios and operator dimensions are preserved; $\beta=1\leftrightarrow
   \beta=0$ is a special case.
4. **Instability** (§2.4): $g_3(h)=1$ has complex roots $h=\frac12+ik$ exactly when $3\beta^2-3\beta>0$, i.e.
   $\beta>1$ or $\beta<0$, so the $O_3$ channel acquires a complex dimension and the conformal solution is
   unstable there. $g_1,g_2,g_4$ never have complex roots.

## Limitations

Undergraduate thesis; large-$N$ only; no finite-$N$ checks; Majorana, non-supersymmetric; no treatment of the
gauged (singlet) sector; no spectral or chaos data beyond what melonic kernels give.

## Relationship to our project

**Is our model covered by this class? No.** The two families differ in every structural respect:

| | Kim 2018 | our $(p,q,N)$ family |
|---|---|---|
| fermions | real Majorana | complex |
| matter | rank-3 tensor, trifundamental of $O(N)^3$ | rank-2 matrix, adjoint of $U(N)$ |
| dynamics | quartic tetrahedral $H$, not SUSY | nilpotent $q$-form supercharge, $H=\{Q,\bar Q\}$ |
| large-$N$ limit | melonic ($g^2N^3$ fixed) | planar / 't Hooft |
| object computed | propagator, 4-pt kernels, dimensions | BPS cohomology, sector energies |
| methods | Schwinger–Dyson at large $N$ | exact cohomology and bootstrap at finite $N$ |

There is no supercharge, BPS state, R-charge or cohomology in the thesis, so **none of its results overlap with
ours directly**. The bridge between the two worlds is KMPT 2018 §6, where the $O(N_1)\times O(N_2)\times O(N_3)$
tensor model at $N_3=1,2$ reduces to a fermionic matrix model; Kim's model is not in that corner.

**Conceptual overlaps (partial, not results):**
1. *Coupling space modulo flavour rotations.* Kim's $\beta\to\frac{1-\beta}{3\beta+1}$ duality is an instance of
   the principle we use when saying that at $p=q=3$ the slot coupling is unique up to scale because
   $\dim\Lambda^3\mathbb C^3=1$, and that generic couplings are optimal: physics lives on orbits of the flavour
   group acting on the coupling tensor. Same principle, different use.
2. *Index loops as the organising device.* Kim uses $N^{\#\text{loops}}$ to prove melonic dominance; we use the
   closed-walk structure of index loops to show only self-loops survive at the maximal weight. Same diagrammatics,
   deployed for a different question.
3. *Melonic SD equations reach what our methods do not.* The IR propagator and kernel spectrum are exactly the
   large-$N$ data that control the near-conformal (Schwarzian) regime, which is the density-of-states question our
   bootstrap does not yet reach. The technology does **not** transfer to our model, which is planar rather than
   melonic, but it is the reason tensor models are attractive as disorder-free SYK analogues.

**New observations prompted by this paper (our own, not in Kim; recorded as hypotheses):**
- *Tensor matter evades both of our obstructions at even $N$.* Our no-go runs through the zero-weight (Cartan)
  sector: self-loops at the maximal weight give the rank law, and the Cartan excess over the positive-root half
  drives the critical-set bound. The adjoint has $r$ zero weights. The trifundamental of $O(N)^3$ has weights
  $(w_1,w_2,w_3)$ with each $w_i\in\{\pm e_j\}$ (plus $0$ only for odd $N$), so **for even $N$ it has no zero
  weights at all**: the positive-weight half gives only $\alpha\ge n/2$, the critical-set bound is vacuous, and the
  maximal-weight space is one-dimensional, so the rank-law mechanism is absent. Neither of our arguments forbids
  concentration for tensor matter.
- *But an odd-degree supercharge cannot be built from trifundamentals alone under $O(N)^3$.* A product of $q$
  tensors has $q$ indices of each of the three types, and $O(N)$ invariants pair indices, so $q$ must be even.
  Our nilpotency requires $q$ odd (a $q$-form with $\omega\wedge\omega=0$). So an $\mathcal N=2$ tensor analogue of
  our family needs mixed field content, or $SU(N)$ epsilon contractions (forcing $q\equiv0\bmod N$ per factor, the
  baryon route we already found unfavourable for fundamentals). The tetrahedral interaction is quartic for exactly
  this parity reason.
- A disorder-free supersymmetric tensor model does exist: Peng–Spradlin–Volovich 2016, now in `papers/` and
  reviewed in `peng_spradlin_volovich_2016.md`. They state the same parity obstruction explicitly (p. 8) and add
  "meson" tensor superfields to evade it, so the observation above should be attributed to them. Their model is
  $\mathcal N=1$: no nilpotent $Q$, no R-charge grading.
