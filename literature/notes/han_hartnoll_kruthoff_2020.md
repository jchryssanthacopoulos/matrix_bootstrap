# Han, Hartnoll, Kruthoff (2020) — Bootstrapping Matrix Quantum Mechanics

**File:** `papers/han_hartnoll_kruthoff_2020.pdf`

## Full citation

Xizhi Han, Sean A. Hartnoll, Jorrit Kruthoff, *Bootstrapping Matrix Quantum Mechanics*, Phys. Rev. Lett. 125 (2020) 041601, arXiv:2004.10212v2 [hep-th] (24 Jul 2020). Code: `github.com/hanxzh94/matrix-bootstrap`.

## Main question

Can the spectrum and simple expectation values of large-$N$ matrix quantum mechanics be obtained numerically by relating expectation values through symmetries (time translation, $SU(N)$ gauge invariance, cyclicity) and bounding them with positivity — i.e. can Lin's matrix-integral bootstrap be lifted to the Hamiltonian setting? This is the paper that introduced the quantum-mechanical matrix bootstrap.

## Physical system

1. Anharmonic oscillator $H=p^2+x^2+gx^4$ (1).
2. One-matrix QM $H=\mathrm{tr}\,P^2+\mathrm{tr}\,X^2+\frac gN\mathrm{tr}\,X^4$ (8), $[P_{ij},X_{kl}]=-i\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}$, solvable by free fermions.
3. Two-matrix QM $H=\mathrm{tr}(P_X^2+P_Y^2+m^2(X^2+Y^2)-g^2[X,Y]^2)$ (17) with $O(2)$ symmetry generated by $S=\mathrm{tr}(XP_Y-YP_X)$ (18); not solvable.

## Important definitions

- Expectation values $\langle O\rangle=\mathrm{tr}\rho O$ for a state with $[\rho,H]=0$ (eigenstate or thermal).
- **Stationarity** $\langle[H,O]\rangle=0$ (2)/(9); the stronger eigenstate relation $\langle OH\rangle=E\langle O\rangle$ (used for the oscillator, (5)) is deliberately avoided in the matrix case because at infinite $N$ the spectrum is continuous.
- **Positivity** $\langle O^\dagger O\rangle\ge0$ for $O=\sum c_ix^i$ ⇔ Hankel matrix $M_{ij}=\langle x^{i+j}\rangle\succeq0$ (7); matrix case: $M=\langle\mathrm{tr}\,O_i^\dagger O_j\rangle$ over strings of $X,P$ of length $\le L$ (15).
- **Gauge constraint** $\langle\mathrm{tr}\,GO\rangle=0$ with $G=i[X,P]+N\mathbf 1$ (11)–(12); e.g. $\langle\mathrm{tr}XP\rangle=-\langle\mathrm{tr}PX\rangle=iN^2/2$ (13).
- **Cyclicity + factorisation**: $\langle\mathrm{tr}XP^3\rangle=\langle\mathrm{tr}P^3X\rangle+2iN\langle\mathrm{tr}P^2\rangle+i\langle\mathrm{tr}P\rangle^2$ (14); general form (S37)–(S38): moving a letter around the trace produces double traces, replaced by products at large $N$ — the quadratic constraints.
- Reality $\langle O^\dagger\rangle=\langle O\rangle^*$; parity/oddness selection rules; $N$-scaling $N^{l/2+1}$ for a length-$l$ single trace.
- Footnote [20], **convergence**: if $\langle1\rangle=1$, $\langle O^\dagger\rangle=\langle O\rangle^*$ and $\langle O^\dagger O\rangle\ge0$ for *all* $O$, then $\langle O\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(\rho O)$ for a genuine state; adding $\langle OH\rangle=E\langle O\rangle$ forces an eigenstate. So as $K\to\infty$ the allowed region shrinks to the exact spectrum.

## Main assumptions

Large-$N$ factorisation of double traces (matrix cases); gauge-singlet states; for the two-matrix case a rotationally invariant ground state ($\langle[S,O]\rangle=0$, (19)). Non-convex constraints solved by a local method (trust-region sequential SDP), so matrix results are local minima of $\langle H\rangle$ in the allowed region — lower bounds if the search finds the global minimum.

## Main analytical results

- Oscillator recursion (6): all $\langle x^t\rangle$ from $E$ and $\langle x^2\rangle$; islands converge to eigenvalues (Fig. 1).
- One-matrix mini-bootstrap (15)–(16): $\langle\mathrm{tr}X^2\rangle\ge0$, $N\langle\mathrm{tr}X^4\rangle\ge\langle\mathrm{tr}X^2\rangle^2$, $\langle\mathrm{tr}X^2\rangle(\langle\mathrm{tr}X^2\rangle+\frac{2g}N\langle\mathrm{tr}X^4\rangle)\ge N^4/4$; saturated at $g=0$.
- Two-matrix virial $E_0=2m^2\langle\mathrm{tr}(X^2+Y^2)\rangle-3g^2\langle\mathrm{tr}[X,Y]^2\rangle$; at large $g$, $E_0/N^2\approx1.40(Ng^2)^{1/3}$ matching Monte Carlo; $N\mathrm{tr}[X,Y]^2/(\mathrm{tr}X^2)^2\to$ const (non-commuting ground state, unlike the two-matrix integral).
- Born–Oppenheimer upper and lower bounds (S13)–(S14), (S29), with perturbative checks (S30)–(S32).

## Numerical methods

Scan over $(E,\langle x^2\rangle)$ with eigenvalue tests (oscillator); for matrices, symbolic reduction of linear constraints to independent variables, then trust-region sequential SDP for the non-convex quadratic constraints; $L\le3$ (one matrix), $L\le4$ (two matrices, $\sim4^{2L}$ raw variables); $O(2)$-charge basis (S39) to prune strings.

## Limitations

Low levels; non-convex solver (no rigorous global bound in the matrix case); bosonic only; no ground-state positivity (later work).

## Relationship to our project

The founding reference for everything in Strand B: every constraint we use (positivity, $\langle[H,O]\rangle=0$, gauge, cyclicity/factorisation, reality) is written here first, and footnote [20] is the cleanest statement of *why* the bootstrap converges — an argument that applies verbatim, and becomes a finite statement, for our finite-dimensional fermionic Hilbert spaces (impose positivity for all words up to the maximal length and the functional is a genuine state; add $\langle OH\rangle=E\langle O\rangle$ and it is an eigenstate). Also the source of the remark that supersymmetry generators should give stronger relations — realised later by Lin–Zheng.
