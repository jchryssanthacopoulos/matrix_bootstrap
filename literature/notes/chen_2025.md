# Chen (2025) — Fortuity with a Single Matrix

**File:** `papers/chen_2025.pdf`

## Full citation

Yiming Chen, *Fortuity with a Single Matrix*, arXiv:2511.00790v2 [hep-th] (11 Nov 2025). Leinweber Institute for Theoretical Physics, Stanford.

## Main question

What is the minimal set of ingredients that produces *fortuitous* BPS states? The paper shows that a single $U(N)$-adjoint fermionic matrix with a cubic supercharge already does, constructs the fortuitous states exactly at finite $N$, and uses a unitary matrix integral to "follow $N$" and watch the states disappear when $N \to N-1$.

## Physical system

$N^2$ complex fermions organised as an $N\times N$ matrix $\Psi=(\Psi_{ij})$ with

$$\{\Psi_{ij},\bar\Psi_{kl}\}=\delta_{il}\delta_{jk},\qquad \{\Psi_{ij},\Psi_{kl}\}=\{\bar\Psi_{ij},\bar\Psi_{kl}\}=0,\qquad \bar\Psi_{ji}\equiv\Psi^\dagger_{ij}\quad\text{(1.1)}$$

$\Psi_{ij}$ are creation operators, $\bar\Psi$ annihilation operators. The model is defined by an $\mathcal N=2$ supercharge

$$Q=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi^3],\qquad H=\{Q,\bar Q\}\quad\text{(1.2)}$$

Hilbert space dimension $2^{N^2}$ (finite). The model is a special instance of the $\mathcal N=2$ SUSY SYK model with non-random, highly sparse couplings (p. 2). The **ungauged** model is studied (the singlet sector has no fortuitous states; p. 5).

Discrete facts: footnote 2 — the alternative model $Q=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi^2\bar\Psi]$ has **no** fortuitous states. Footnote 3 — generalisations $Q=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi^p]$, $p$ odd, were not studied.

## Important definitions

- **Adjoint basis** (2.1): $\Psi=\frac{\psi^0}{\sqrt N}\mathbf 1+\sum_{a=1}^{N^2-1}\sqrt2\,\psi^aT^a$, with $\mathrm{Tr}[T^aT^b]=\tfrac12\delta^{ab}$, $[T^a,T^b]=if^{abc}T^c$ (2.2). Then $\{\psi^a,\bar\psi^b\}=\delta^{ab}$ and
  $$Q=\tfrac{i}{\sqrt2}f^{abc}\psi^a\psi^b\psi^c\quad\text{(2.3)}$$
  The trace mode $\psi^0$ decouples; it only doubles every degeneracy.
- **R-charge / fermion number** $N_\Psi=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi\bar\Psi]$ with $[N_\Psi,Q]=3Q$ (2.4). $\Psi\to U\Psi U^\dagger$ under the global $SU(N)$ (2.5).
- **$su(N)$ generators** $J^a=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi[T^a,\bar\Psi]]=-if^{abc}\psi^b\bar\psi^c$, $[J^a,J^b]=if^{abc}J^c$ (2.6); quadratic Casimir $\hat C_2=\sum_a J^aJ^a$ (2.7).
- **Positive-root generators** $J_{E^{ij}}=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi[E^{ij},\bar\Psi]]$, $i<j$ (2.10), Cartan $J_{H_i}$ (2.12); action on fermions (2.13)–(2.14). Lower-triangle $\Psi_{ij}$ ($i>j$) carry positive roots.
- **Maximal representation $r_*$**: the $SU(N)$ irrep of largest $C_2$ appearing in $\wedge^p\,\mathbf{adj}$ (2.9). Highest-weight state
  $$|\lambda\rangle=\prod_{i>j}\Psi_{ij}|0\rangle,\qquad\lambda=(2,2,\dots,2)\ (N{-}1\text{ entries})\quad\text{(2.15)–(2.16)}$$
  and the family $|\tilde\lambda\rangle=\prod_{\text{any }i}\Psi_{ii}\prod_{i>j}\Psi_{ij}|0\rangle$ (2.17). Young diagram: staircase with $2N-2i$ boxes in row $i$, $N-1$ rows (Fig. 3).
- **Fortuity** (as in Chang–Chen–Sia–Yang): the paper defers the formal definition to [12] (=arXiv:2412.06902) and uses the criterion that BPS states whose charge range depends explicitly on $N$ must be fortuitous (§2.2).
- **"Following $N$"** (§3.2): a penalty factor $\lambda\in[0,1]$ multiplies the fugacity of the last row/column fermions $\Psi_{iN},\Psi_{Ni}$ in the unitary matrix integral (3.18), interpolating between the $N$ and $N-1$ theories.

## Main assumptions

- Ungauged model; global $U(N)=U(1)\times SU(N)$.
- Large-$N$ saddle analysis in §3 is only under control at the special fugacities $q=e^{\pm i\pi/3}$; general $q$ is speculative (§3.3).
- The claims about the 3-matrix model in §4 rest on an (unpublished, unquantified) "numerical simulation" at $N=2$.

## Main analytical results

1. **Exact solvability**: $H=\{Q,\bar Q\}=3N(N^2-1)-9\hat C_2$ (2.8). Spectrum depends only on the $SU(N)$ Casimir ⇒ non-chaotic. Singlets ($C_2=0$) are the **highest**-energy states; zero-energy states are those of **maximal** Casimir.
2. **Maximal representation**: $C_2(r_*)=N(N^2-1)/3$ (2.18), which exactly cancels the constant in (2.8) ⇒ $E=0$; $\dim r_*=3^{N(N-1)/2}$ (2.19). Casimir formula from Young diagram: (A.5)–(A.6). Character $\chi_{r_*}(U)=\prod_{i<j}(1+2\cos(\theta_i-\theta_j))$ (3.3)/(A.3).
3. **BPS partition function**
   $$Z_{\rm BPS}(q)=\mathrm{Tr}_{E=0}[q^{N_\Psi}]=3^{N(N-1)/2}(1+q)^Nq^{N(N-1)/2}\quad\text{(2.20)},\qquad Z_{r_*}(q)=(1+q)^Nq^{N(N-1)/2}\quad\text{(2.21)}$$
   so BPS states exist iff $\tfrac{N(N-1)}2\le N_\Psi\le\tfrac{N(N+1)}2$ (2.22). Entropy is $O(N^2)$ but only because $r_*$ is large; the *multiplicity* of $r_*$ does not grow as $e^{N^2}$.
4. **No R-charge concentration**: (2.22) spans $N+1$ charge sectors, violating the concentration criterion of [12] (a single cochain with exponentially many states per irreducible complex). Comparison: generic $\mathcal N=2$ SYK with $N^2$ fermions has BPS states only in $N^2/2-1\le N_\Psi\le N^2/2+1$ (p. 8, $N$ even). Explanation: "the $SU(N)$ symmetry constrains the interaction so severely that not all the states that can be lifted are lifted."
5. **Fortuity of $r_*$** (§2.2): the staircase diagram has the maximal allowed $N-1$ rows; for $N<N_*$ (Fig. 4c) it is disallowed ⇒ the states become null. For $N>N_*$ (Fig. 4a) the weight is $(2,\dots,2,0,\dots,0)$ — non-maximal ⇒ not BPS. Sharp sensitivity to $N$ (Fig. 1) even without concentration.
6. **Unitary matrix integral** (§3): $Z_{r_*}(q)=\int DU\,\chi_{r_*}(U)\exp[\sum_n\frac{(-1)^{n+1}q^n}{n}\mathrm{Tr}U^n\mathrm{Tr}U^{\dagger n}]$ (3.2). Exponentiating the character gives a double-trace model with complex coefficients $a_n=(-1)^{n+1}q^n-\frac n\pi V_{r_*,n}$, $V_{r_*,n}=\frac\pi n e^{i2\pi n/3}$ (3.7)–(3.8). At $q=e^{\pm i\pi/3}$ the integrand becomes a Vandermonde in $3\theta$ (3.11); saddles are $\theta_i=\frac{2\pi i}{3N}+\frac{2\pi}{3}\alpha_i$, $\alpha_i\in\{0,1,2\}$ (3.13), a family of $3^N$ configurations all with zero action, reproducing $Z_{r_*}(e^{i\pi/3})\approx e^{i\pi N^2/6}$ (3.15). Fourier modes $\rho_{3n-2},\rho_{3n-1}$ are massless, $\rho_{3n}$ massive (3.16)–(3.17).
7. **Following $N$**: exact small-$N$ results $\tilde Z_{r_*}(q,\lambda)=\lambda^{N-1}(1+\lambda q)(1+q)^{N-1}q^{N(N-1)/2}$ for $N=3,4,5$ (3.19). At $q=e^{i\pi/3}$ the $\theta_N$ integral does *not* kill individual saddles (3.24)/(B.2); vanishing comes from cancellation among the $2\pi/3$-shifted saddles: averaging over shifts gives $\tfrac13\sum_{\alpha}f(\theta_{iN}+\tfrac{2\pi}3\alpha)=\tilde\lambda|e^{i3\theta_i}-e^{i3\theta_N}|^2$ (3.28) ⇒ $\tilde Z\propto\tilde\lambda^{N-1}Z_{r_*}$ (3.29). "Phantom" saddles [12].
8. **Two-matrix generalisation** (4.1): $Q=\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi_1^3]+\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi_2^3]$ gives *singlet* fortuitous states via $\mathbf 1\subset r_{*,\Psi_1}\otimes r_{*,\Psi_2}$ (4.2), but not exponentially many and no concentration.
9. **Matrix SYK proposal** (§4, "Simplest chaotic matrix model with R-charge concentration"): among purely fermionic matrix models with cubic supercharges ("matrix SYK models") "the magic number might be three". Example
   $$Q=\sum_{1\le i\le j\le k\le3}\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi_i\Psi_j\Psi_k]\quad\text{(4.3)}$$
   at $N=2$ (12 complex fermions) "suggests R-charge concentration" numerically. Such a model "will likely have the super-Schwarzian [49] as its low energy effective theory." The paper explicitly suggests the quantum mechanical matrix bootstrap [55,56] (= Lin–Zheng 2025, Laliberte–McPeak 2025) as a tool. No data, no chaos diagnostics, no larger $N$ are given.

## Limitations

- Solvable ⇒ non-chaotic, non-holographic; captures the "skeleton" of fortuity, not its "meat" (no R-charge concentration).
- Large-$N$ matrix-integral analysis only controlled at $q=e^{\pm i\pi/3}$; for generic $q$ the truncation method of [29] did not show convergence (§3.3).
- No statement about the 3-matrix model beyond $N=2$ and beyond "suggests concentration".
- Ungauged; the interesting states are non-singlet, so standard singlet-sector large-$N$ technology does not apply directly.

## Relationship to our project

This is the **defining paper** for the project. It (i) supplies the exactly solvable single-matrix model that serves as our validation target ($E_0(N_\Psi)=3N(N^2-1)-9C_2^{\max}(N_\Psi)$, BPS window (2.22), degeneracies (2.20)), (ii) proposes the 3-matrix cubic model (4.3) as the simplest candidate chaotic, R-charge-concentrating matrix model, and (iii) explicitly nominates the matrix bootstrap as the method. Our central question — does the BPS window of (4.3) stay $O(1)$ in width as $N$ grows while the single-matrix window grows like $N+1$ — is the sharp version of Chen's unquantified remark. The "following $N$" idea (penalising the last row/column) is a natural probe of fortuity that a bootstrap could mimic by tracking bounds as a function of $N$.

Flagged gaps: no numbers for the $N=2$ 3-matrix simulation are published; the claim of super-Schwarzian low-energy dynamics is a conjecture; whether (4.3) is chaotic is asserted only as an expectation.
