# Research questions

*Last updated 2026-09-15 (after the literature review; see `literature/synthesis.md`). Keep this file current.*

## Primary question

**Q1. Does Chen's 3-matrix "matrix SYK" model exhibit R-charge concentration for $N\ge3$?**

Model: $p=3$ $U(N)$-adjoint complex fermion matrices, $Q=\sum_{1\le i\le j\le k\le3}\mathrm{Tr}[\Psi_i\Psi_j\Psi_k]$, $H=\{Q,\bar Q\}$ (Chen 2025, eq. 4.3). Chen reports only that an $N=2$ simulation "suggests" concentration.

Sharp formulation (Chang–Chen–Sia–Yang 2024, Conjecture 1 and §2): in every irreducible cochain complex — labelled by $N_\Psi$ mod 3, the $SU(N)$ irrep, and flavor quantum numbers (the flavour symmetry of Chen's $C$ is $\mathbb Z_3=A_3$, **not** $S_3$: the transpositions are not symmetries — verified 2026-09-23, note §4h) — with macroscopic index, BPS states occupy a *single* degree. Observable proxy: the sector-resolved ground energy $E_0(N_\Psi,\mathbf r)$; concentration ⇔ $E_0>0$ outside a window of at most three consecutive $N_\Psi$ (one per $\mathbb Z_3$ class) per irrep. Baseline: the single-matrix model has $E_0=0$ on $N+1$ consecutive sectors, all in $r_*$ (Chen (2.22)) — non-concentrated for $N\ge3$.

Sub-questions:
- Q1a. Can the bootstrap give rigorous lower bounds $E_0(N_\Psi)>0$ in lifted sectors at $N$ beyond exact diagonalisation ($N=3$: $2^{27}$ states)?
- Q1b. Are refined indices $\mathrm{Tr}[(-1)^Fq^{N_\Psi}]$ per $SU(N)$ irrep saturated by BPS counts (index saturation ⇔ concentration, Chang et al. §5.1)?
- Q1c. How does the number of BPS states scale with $N$ ($e^{cN^2}$ multiplicity, as for a genuine black-hole-like sector — Chang–Lin 2024 Conjecture 3 — versus a large irrep with small multiplicity as in the single-matrix model)?
- Q1d. **Do the BPS counts in the three charges of a concentrated complex stay in ratio $1{:}2{:}1$ as $N$ grows?** This is the $\hat q=3$ super-Schwarzian prediction $N_{\rm BPS}(k)\propto\cos(\pi k/3)$ (Turiaci–Witten (3.10)), realised exactly in $\mathcal N=2$ SYK (FGMS (5.7)), and matched by the project's $243{:}486{:}243$ at $N=2$; the single-matrix binomial weights depart from it for $N\ge3$.

## Secondary questions

**Q2. Is the 3-matrix model chaotic?** Primary diagnostic: the $r$-ratio statistics of the *singular values of $Q_k$* between adjacent charge sectors $(k,k+3)$ at fixed $SU(N)$ irrep, compared with the Altland–Zirnbauer $\beta=2$ surmise (Turiaci–Witten §2.5; $\beta=1$ for the $CT$-self-conjugate multiplet at odd fermion number). Secondary: level statistics of $H$ in symmetry-resolved sectors at the largest accessible $N$; LMRS statistics of projected simple operators in the BPS subspace; information/entanglement entropy of BPS states (Chang et al. §3.4); whether gaps close as $N\to\infty$ (Casimir-type fermionic matrix models keep $O(1)$ gaps, Klebanov et al. 2018 §6.1).

**Q3. What is the minimal structure that breaks the Casimir shortcut?** Which cubic supercharges (number of flavors, which trace structures) have $H$ *not* a function of Casimirs? Is $p=3$ really the "magic number"? Do $p=2$ models with mixed traces already concentrate?

**Q4. Large-$N$ organisation of the fortuitous sector.** Fortuitous states are maximal-Casimir, non-singlet (single matrix) or partly singlet (3-matrix, per project ED at $N=2$). Does large-$N$ factorisation of single-trace correlators hold in the relevant states? If not, what replaces it (covariant/Wigner–Eckart blocks, Marchesini–Onofri-type non-singlet equations)?

**Q5. Near-BPS spectrum and the super-Schwarzian.** Quantitative targets from Turiaci–Witten (3.11) and FGMS (5.39)–(5.42): non-BPS multiplet gaps $E_0(q)=q^2/(4\hat q^2)$ in Schwarzian units (growing quadratically with distance of the average charge $q$ from the window), edge density $\rho_q\propto\sinh(2\pi\sqrt{E-E_0(q)})/E$, and $1/\sqrt\varepsilon$ threshold only for the $q=0$ multiplet. Unknown for the matrix model: the overall Schwarzian scale as a function of $N$ (is it $\propto1/N^2$, the natural guess with $N^2$ fermions?). (Chen's conjecture; untested.)

**Q6. Methodological.** For a purely fermionic model the operator algebra truncates; what is the smallest operator level at which the bootstrap is tight in (a) BPS sectors, (b) lifted sectors, as a function of $N$? Which constraints are essential (supercharge EOM, ground-state positivity restricted to sector-preserving operators, $SU(N)$ Ward identities)?

## Status of what is known

*Re-verified 2026-09-15 in the project `.venv` unless stated; see `docs/todo.md` for the two corrections found.*

- Single matrix: $E_0(N_\Psi)$ exact for $N\le4$; BPS window $[N(N-1)/2,N(N+1)/2]$ confirmed; covariant single-trace bootstrap tight at $N=2$ in all sectors and at $N=3$ in BPS sectors; lifted sectors at $N=3$ not yet tight.
- 3-matrix, $N=2$: 972 BPS states at $N_\Psi\in\{5,6,7\}$ with counts $243:486:243$; 168 distinct energies; gauge-singlet BPS multiplets present.
- 2026-09-16: sector-resolved bootstrap with adjoint-valued operators (D3) is exact for the single-matrix model in all sectors at $N=3$ and in 15/17 sectors at $N=4$; the window-edge sectors converge slowly in level ($0\to11.4\to14.7$ of $18$ at levels $\le3,4,5$). Three-matrix $N=2$: $k=0,1$ exact (analytic anchors), $k=2,3$ at the SUSY floor at level 2; level 3 feasible only for $k=2$ within memory.
- 2026-09-17: symmetry-reduced bootstrap (D4: invariant $\rho_k$ over $SU(2)_{\rm gauge}\times\mathbb Z_3$ isotypic blocks, $\mathbb Z_3$-graded cones, Cho et al. adjoint-projected channel). Three-matrix $N=2$: $E_0(2)=5.16536$ **reproduced exactly at level 3** (the plain adjoint channel stalls at 88 %); $k=3$: $E_0(3)\ge0.9622$ at level 3 (78 %, 9 GB) and **exact ($1.22706$) at level 4** (18 GB, at the memory ceiling); $k=4$ (almost BPS, $E_0=0.088$) not lifted at level 3. Partial answer to Q6: at $N=2$ the essential ingredients in lifted sectors are the sector restriction, the adjoint-valued words, the EOM and the singlet/adjoint split of the Gram matrix; Ward identities are automatic and GS positivity is not needed. Q1a status and plan (2026-09-18): the Hilbert-space bootstrap stops at $N=2$ by design ($d_k$ up to $2\times10^7$ at $N=3$). The plan of record (`docs/trace_bootstrap_plan.md`) re-expresses the same, now-validated constraint set at the trace level with $N$ as a numerical coefficient (exact at finite $N$, no factorisation); realistic reach is the fixed-$k$ ($k\lesssim4$–$5$), any-$N$ regime, where $k=0,1$ are the D2 anchors and $k\ge2$ is new. The half-filling window — the sector that decides concentration, i.e. the first lifted sector next to the BPS window, with many particles and a tiny gap ($k=4$ at $N=2$: $E_0=0.088$, unlifted at level 3) — is where every bootstrap is weakest; at $N=3$ it is a sparse-Lanczos problem (plan §8), at $N\ge4$ neither method reaches it and index/cohomology counting remains the tool. Concentration is a $1/N$-subleading effect on $O(N^3)$ energies, so the planar ('t Hooft) bootstrap is blind to it.
- 2026-09-18 (trace bootstrap, `docs/trace_bootstrap_plan.md`, results in `research/notes/trace_bootstrap_results.md`): exact-finite-$N$ trace engine built and validated (reproduces the $N=2$ Hilbert-space values once the length-6 finite-$N$ relations are included; D2 anchors to $10^{-8}$ at $N\le30$). New: for $k\le N-1$ the $k$-particle sector is **non-interacting**, $E_0(k;N)=16N^3-(15+38k)N$ (rigorous level-2 bounds tight against few-body ED for $k=2,3$ up to $N=10$–$100$); the first interacting sector is $k=N$, with $E_0(3;3)\ge70.35$ (exact 75.79) at level 3. Consequence for Q5: near-BPS/interacting physics at fixed $k$ begins only at $k\ge N$, so the fixed-$k$ large-$N$ regime is free below that — the interesting sectors at large $N$ are $k\gtrsim N$, one order further than the level ceiling allows.
- 2026-09-19 (refined index, D6, `research/notes/bps_index_results.md`): exact $I_{c,\lambda,\omega}$ for $N=2,3,4$. **Q1b:** saturated in every complex at $N=2$ (index = ED count in all 36 complexes). **Q1c:** total BPS index $\tfrac23 3^{3N^2/2}|\cos(\cdot)|\sim e^{1.65N^2}$, spread over hundreds of irreps with per-irrep multiplicities growing like $e^{0.75N^2}$ ($18,\,891,\,3.5\times10^5$) including gauge singlets ($6,81,1944$) — versus a single maximal-Casimir irrep with $O(1)$ multiplicity and no singlets in the single-matrix model. **Q1d:** the class profile is exactly Turiaci–Witten's $\cos(\pi(k-k_*)/3)$: $2{:}1{:}1$ (even $N$, three-sector window) and $0{:}1{:}1$ (odd $N$, two-sector window $\{k_*\pm\tfrac12\}$), kinematically at every $N$ given saturation; at $N=3$ the index vanishes in every $k\equiv0$ complex. The index does not locate the degrees; that is what the bootstrap exclusion test (or cohomology ranks) must supply.
- 2026-09-22 (**exact $Q$-cohomology**, D8, `src/cohomology.py`, `research/notes/cohomology_results.md`): the $U(N)$ weight decomposition splits the complex into spaces of dimension $\le126$ instead of $2\times10^7$, giving exact BPS counts for the eight highest irreps at $N=3$ ($C_2\ge35$) in under two minutes, validated against ED ($N=2$), the D6 multiplicities, and the refined index in all 27 flavour-refined complexes. Cohomology is supported on exactly $k\in\{12,13,14,15\}$ (width 4, centred on half filling $13.5$).
  - **Q1b — amended.** Saturation is *not* universal once $N>2$. At $N=3$ the $c=1$ and $c=2$ complexes are exactly saturated (cohomology in a single degree, $k=13$ and $k=14$), so concentration holds there; but every $c=0$ complex has vanishing index and non-zero cohomology, split in cancelling pairs between $k=12$ and $k=15$. That is $149{,}526$ index-invisible BPS states, $19.7\%$ of the $758{,}808$ total in these irreps. Not a counterexample to CCSY, whose conjecture is conditioned on a macroscopic index — but it means the index is a lower bound on the BPS count, not a proxy for it, at finite $N$.
  - **Q1d — amended.** The Turiaci–Witten $\cos$ profile describes the *index* correctly at $N=3$ ($0{:}1{:}1$ over $c=0,1,2$), but not the *BPS counts*, which are $149{,}526:304{,}641:304{,}641\approx1{:}2{:}2$. At $N=2$ the cohomology independently reproduces $243{:}486{:}243$, irrep by irrep. **Conjecture** (two data points, stated as such): window width $N+1$ centred at $pN^2/2$, degree profile $\binom{N}{j}$ — already failing per-irrep at $(5,0,-5)$, where it is $38{:}486{:}486{:}38$ rather than $1{:}3{:}3{:}1$; $N=4$ is the first real test.
  - **Bootstrap calibration.** Grading every $N=3$ exclusion cell against exact truth: zero contradictions; at $C_2=48$ the level-3 frontier is exactly sharp (excludes $k\le11$, inconclusive from $k=12$, where BPS states truly begin); at $C_2=42,38,35$ it falls 3–4 degrees short of the truth.
- 2026-09-23 (**$N=4$ by the same method**, D8.5): the maximal weight $(9,3,-3,-9)$ runs in 6 s at 0.33 GB — against $\dim\mathcal H_{24}=3.2\times10^{13}$ — after replacing the basis enumerator with a pruned recursion. $h^{22..26}=81,324,486,324,81=81\binom4j$.
  - **Q1d — the conjecture holds at $N=4$.** Window width $N+1$ centred on $k=pN^2/2$ with profile $\binom Nj$, now verified at $N=2,3,4$ (though only at the high-Casimir end for $N\ge3$).
  - **Q1b — sharpened, and worse than at $N=3$.** At $N=3$ saturation failed only in the $c=0$ complexes, which have zero index, so the index remained reliable wherever it was non-zero. At $N=4$ that escape closes: the $c=1$ and $c=2$ complexes have index $-81$ per flavour charge and cohomology in *two* degrees, $135$ multiplets against $|I|=81$; only $c=0$ saturates. Over the irrep, $1296$ multiplets versus $972$ from the index — a $25\%$ excess, against $19.7\%$ at $N=3$. **Index saturation is not a reliable finite-$N$ proxy for BPS counts even when the index is macroscopic.** This does not refute CCSY (asymptotic in $N$), but it rules out finite-$N$ index arguments as a substitute for counting.
- 2026-09-23b (**$N=4$ to 13 irreps**, D8.6, note §4d): a blocked BLAS rank (exact via a prime with $b(P-1)^2<2^{53}$) plus $\mathbb Z_3$ flavour blocking over $\mathbb F_P$, $P\equiv1\bmod3$, take the largest tractable weight space from 924 to 30 294 — all 13 irreps with $C_2\ge107$ in ~30 min at 6.1 GB. Exact count: $3\,008\,825\,568$ BPS states, $19.0\%$ index-invisible.
  - **Q1b.** The $N=4$ failure of saturation is the rule, not an artefact of the maximal weight: in all 13 irreps $c=0$ is saturated at $k=24$ while $c=1,2$ occupy two degrees each with non-vanishing index.
  - **Q1d — the profile half is false.** $\binom Nj$ holds for 11 of 13 but breaks at $C_2=107$ ($114,1572,2916,1572,114$), mirroring $(5,0,-5)$ at $N=3$; in both the centre is unchanged and the window edges are suppressed. The **width** half survives: every one of the 25 irreps computed at $N=2,3,4$ has its BPS states in exactly $N+1$ consecutive degrees centred on $k=pN^2/2$. Since concentration is a statement about width, this is the half that matters — but it is still a conjecture, resting entirely on the high-Casimir top of each spectrum.
- 2026-09-23c (**$N=5$**, note §4f): maximal weight in 49 s; window $k=35..40$ (width $N+1$), profile $3^5\binom5j$, all three classes with two degrees so none saturates — §4e's prediction for $N\ge5$ confirmed.
  - **Q1 — a negative answer at the top of the spectrum.** At $N=2,3,4,5$ the maximal irrep has $Z_{\rm BPS}=3^N(1+q)^Nq^{k_{\min}}$. The $(1+q)^N$ is identical to Chen's single-matrix (2.20), the case Chen (2.22) cites as having no R-charge concentration; and the $N+1$ window directly violates the Q1 criterion ("at most three consecutive $N_\Psi$"). So Chen's hope that the 3-matrix model concentrates is **not borne out for the maximal irrep** — it behaves exactly like the solvable single-matrix baseline.
  - **But the conjecture is not yet tested.** CCSY condition concentration on macroscopic index. The maximal irrep has $6^N$ multiplets ($e^{1.8N}$), while the largest multiplicity at $N=4$ is $6.6\times10^9$ ($\approx e^{1.4N^2}$) at $C_2=20$. Every $N\ge3$ result so far is in the thin high-Casimir top; raising $N$ extends the pattern without reaching a macroscopic-index complex. The informative move is **down in Casimir at $N=3$** (reachable to $C_2=24$, multiplicity $110\,080$; $C_2=20$ with more memory; $C_2\le15$ out of reach).
- 2026-09-23d (**$N=3$ descent to $C_2=24$**, note §4g): 13 irreps, complete tiers 48→24. **Q1 — the negative answer holds where it counts.** The BPS support is exactly 4 consecutive degrees in every irrep, including $(4,0,-4)$ at Fock multiplicity $110\,080$ ($215\times$ the maximal irrep), so strict concentration fails at the most macroscopic complex reachable, not just at the thin high-Casimir top where all earlier data sat. **Q1b** — the index remains a good proxy for the *count* (96–97% away from the top, where the profile is sharply peaked) while being wrong about the *support* everywhere; the $10.1\%$ aggregate is dominated by the flat-ratio top and is not a spectrum-wide figure. A monotone trend toward vanishing edges seen on the $C_2\ge27$ tiers does **not** persist ($h^{12}$: $38,36,18$ then $64$ at $C_2=24$). Remaining gap: $C_2\le20$, up to the peak multiplicity $511\,488$ at $C_2=8$.
- 2026-09-23e (**analytic no-go**, D9): the maximal-weight sector is solved in closed form. $Q$ reduces to $\omega\wedge-$ on $\Lambda^\bullet\mathbb C^p$ at each of the $r=\mathrm{rank}\,G$ Cartan slots, so $Z_{\max}(t)=t^{k_0}(z_{\rm slot}(t))^r$ — verified exactly against all ten measured $u(N)$ cases. Window width $W=r(w_s-1)+1$ with $w_s$ the support of $z_{\rm slot}$. Since $\sum_c E_c=(1-1)^p=0$ and the $E_c$ are not all zero, $w_s\ge2$ always, so $W\ge r+1$ and concentration ($W\le q$) forces $\mathrm{rank}\,G\le q-1$. Solving $z_{\rm slot}$: $w_s=2$ with $\omega\ne0$ occurs only at $q=3$ ($p\equiv3\bmod4$), and $w_s^{\min}=q-1$ for $q\ge5$, so $r_{\max}=2$ over the whole family. **Answer to Q1, in general: no model in this class concentrates beyond $\mathrm{rank}\,G=2$** — exactly $u(2)$ and $su(3)$, which is why Chen's $N=2$ evidence looked positive. Scaling $p$ yields infinitely many concentrating models but all at rank 2. The only evasion is $q>p$, where $\omega=0$ and $Q$ annihilates the sector — the free/solvable branch, not a chaotic one.
